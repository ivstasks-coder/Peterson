About Peterson Linen

Nobody calls a linen company because they are excited about linen.

They call because three hundred people are arriving on Saturday, the room has to look right, and there is no second try. The color matters. But the part that keeps someone up at night is simpler than that — will it show up, and will it be right when it does.

That is the part we are good at.

What we do

We rent and sell linen for events. Tablecloths, napkins, runners, chair covers, chair ties, skirting. Solids, satins, damask, taffeta. More than seventy napkin colors on the shelf.

Weddings, showers, bar and bat mitzvahs, anniversaries, birthdays. Corporate dinners, seminars, expos, office parties. Events from fifty guests up to seven hundred and fifty.

We are small, and that is the point

There are seven of us. Five spend their days sorting, washing, ironing and inspecting linen. One full-time driver, one part-time. Every sash is tied by hand. Every piece is checked more than once before it leaves the building, and Pam checks a good deal of it herself.

When you call, you get Pam. Not a call center, not an account rep who changes every year. She takes the order, she plans the delivery, and she is the one who picks up when your count changes three days out — which it does, and which is fine.

We deliver on Sundays

Most events happen on a weekend. The national companies deliver Monday to Friday. We do not, because that is not when our customers need us.

Two weeks' notice is ideal. If you are in a bind, we can usually turn an order around in three or four days. It is always worth asking.

Where we go

About fifty miles out from Braintree. Boston, the South Shore, Greater Boston, west to Worcester, and the Cape for larger events.

Who we work with

Hotels, function and banquet halls, caterers, restaurants and country clubs, mostly. We work with event planners and wedding coordinators, and we will work alongside your caterer, your florist and your venue so that nobody is guessing on the day.

Peterson Linen is a certified women-owned business through WBENC. If your hotel, university or corporate purchasing office needs that on file, we have it. Certificates of insurance too — just ask, and we will send them the same day.

If you are planning your own event, call us. The first conversation is free.

One rule

Pam's father had one, and nobody has improved on it: his word was his bond, and you do the best you can for the customer.

Peterson Linen has been in Braintree since long before the Collins family owned it. He bought it in 1999, after thirty years in the business. It has been hers since 2005. The rule came with it.

About Pamelyn Collins

Pam Collins did not plan on running a linen company.

She grew up in Worcester, studied economics at Providence College, then law at Suffolk. She practiced in Dedham for close to ten years.

Her father spent more than thirty years in the linen business, and in 1999 he bought Peterson Linen, an established company in Braintree. In 2005 she lost him, and the business became hers.

For a while she did both — the law practice during the week, the linen business around the edges of it. By 2007 it was clear she had to pick one. She picked this one.

It was not the safe choice. The economy fell apart the year after. She stayed.

Ask her why and the answer is short. She would rather build something of her own than work for somebody else.

The law degree turned out to be more useful than she expected. Venue agreements, insurance certificates, vendor contracts, the fine print on a corporate account — she reads all of it herself, and it saves her customers a great deal of trouble.

But most days she is in production. She unloads the machines. She irons. She checks the linen before it goes out the door. All these years later that has not changed, and she does not want it to.

She will tell you the good days are not the quiet ones. They are the ones where a count doubles on a Thursday, or a venue changes a room at the last minute, and it still goes out right.

Away from work there is a thirteen-year-old pit bull, yoga, running, and the ocean whenever she can get to it. In February, somewhere warm.
